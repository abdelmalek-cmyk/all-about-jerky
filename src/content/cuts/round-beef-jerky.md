Top round and bottom round are the workhorses of the beef jerky world. Affordable, lean, and widely available, both cuts produce excellent jerky with predictable results. If eye of round is the precision choice, top round and bottom round are the practical everyday choice — and they're what most experienced home jerky makers have in their freezer.

## Top Round: The Flat-Cut Workhorse

Top round comes from the inner portion of the rear leg (round primal). It's sold as a whole roast (2–4 lbs) or pre-sliced into steaks. The flat shape produces wide, uniform strips — larger than eye of round strips because the muscle is broader.

**Ideal thickness:** 1/4 inch against the grain. The wide flat shape means you can cut multiple strips across the surface of each steak.

**Flavor profile:** Slightly richer and beefier than eye of round. The extra myoglobin in top round adds flavor depth that shows up clearly in simply seasoned recipes like peppered jerky or classic soy-and-pepper.

![Beef sliced thin against the grain on dark cutting board showing uniform strips](/images/beef-jerky-slicing.webp)

## Bottom Round: The Chewier Alternative

Bottom round comes from the outer rear leg. It has a tighter grain and more connective tissue than top round, which translates to:
- **More chew** — good if you prefer jerky with serious bite
- **Must be sliced against the grain** — with-the-grain slices will be very tough
- **Slightly more forgiving on time** — the tighter grain holds moisture a bit longer, giving you a wider window before over-drying

## Yield Comparison

Both top and bottom round yield approximately 55–60% of their raw weight in finished jerky.

| Raw weight | Finished jerky |
|---|---|
| 1 lb | 6–7 oz |
| 2 lbs | 12–14 oz |
| 3 lbs | 18–21 oz |

## Which Round Cut to Choose?

![Various lean beef cuts for jerky on dark butcher paper — eye of round, top round, flank steak comparison](/images/best-meat-for-beef-jerky-cuts.webp)

- **Choose top round** when you want larger, more uniform strips and slightly more flavor
- **Choose bottom round** when top round is unavailable, or when you want chewier jerky
- **Choose eye of round** when you want the absolute leanest, most consistent result

All three are interchangeable in any beef jerky recipe. The marinade and drying method matter far more than which round cut you use.
