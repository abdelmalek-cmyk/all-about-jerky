BBQ beef jerky is the gateway between American barbecue and snack food. It has the same flavor landmarks as a good barbecue sauce ”” smoke, sweetness, tang, and depth ”” packed into a shelf-stable strip that's been concentrated by 5 hours of dehydration.

The secret is **layered sweetness**: honey for floral sweetness, brown sugar for molasses depth, and tomato paste for the savory backbone that prevents it from tasting like candy jerky. The apple cider vinegar provides the brightness that makes commercial BBQ jerky taste flat by comparison.

![Pellet smoker with lid open showing beef jerky strips on grates with smoke wisps](/images/smoked-beef-jerky-smoker.webp)

## Using a Smoker vs. Dehydrator

This recipe works in either, but the smoker version is exceptional:

| Method | Smoke Flavor | Time | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dehydrator + liquid smoke | Mild, clean | 4”“6 hr | Consistent, reliable |
| Pellet smoker (hickory) | Rich, complex | 4”“6 hr | Best BBQ flavor |
| Oven + liquid smoke | Mild | 3”“5 hr | Good, no equipment needed |

For the smoker version: skip the liquid smoke in the marinade and smoke at 160”“180°F with hickory chips.

## Honey BBQ vs. Regular BBQ

![Beef jerky marinade with soy sauce, Worcestershire and BBQ ingredients in zip-lock bag](/images/beef-jerky-marinade-bag.webp)

The **honey BBQ variation** doubles the honey to 3 tablespoons, drops the tomato paste, and adds a pinch more cayenne for a sweeter, hotter profile ”” similar to Honey BBQ Fritos or Bridgford honey BBQ jerky. For **Texas-style BBQ jerky**, increase smoked paprika to 2 tsp, add 1/2 tsp cumin, and use only 1 tbsp honey for a drier, more savory result.
