# Stories Reference

Personal anecdotes, recurring characters, and story patterns the writer uses. Pull from these to add authenticity and warmth to content.

## Recurring characters & personal details
- **The 14-year-old son** — the writer's comedic yardstick for "how embarrassing is this?" Referenced as the reader-embarrassment gauge. Great for self-deprecating asides.
- **The writer's own taste, owned proudly** — loves dad jokes and Nickelback; uses these as authenticity markers and audience filters.
- **Career origin** — "Writing was not my initial career choice, I found it later in life." Establishes a relatable, non-traditional path and humility.

## Signature anecdote patterns
- **"I learned the hard way."** Framing a lesson as personal trial-and-error (LinkedIn: burned Claude Design credits in ~1 hour, then spent an extra $34). High-trust, high-relatability.
- **"I just built X."** Concrete personal proof before giving advice (built a 5-page site in 10 minutes; video hit 32,000 views). Show, then teach.
- **Before/after transformation.** Old painful way vs. new better way (10 hours in WordPress fighting "design gymnastics" → one smooth Claude Design + Code flow).
- **The relatable failure.** Admitting a bit may have bombed ("I may have lost you at the Nickelback comment").

## Story-shaped analogies (reusable)
- **Darts** — learning humor/craft: first you're happy to hit the board, accuracy comes with practice, sometimes you miss entirely.
- **Frank's Red Hot** — on overusing humor: "you can't put that stuff on everything."
- **The dad-joke train** — the writer as your "spirit guide" on the journey.

## Structural role of stories
- **Open with one.** A quick personal moment or bold personal result as the hook.
- **Use as proof.** Anecdote → lesson → actionable takeaway.
- **Keep them short.** A few sentences; the story serves the point, not the reverse.
- **Land the lesson.** Every story resolves into something the reader can use.

## Emotional register
- Warm, humble, occasionally self-mocking. The writer is comfortable being the butt of the joke, which makes the advice land softer and the authority feel earned.

## For food/blog contexts (e.g., jerk seasoning)
Adapt the same patterns: a personal "first time I tasted real jerk" moment, a "I learned the hard way" kitchen mistake (too much Scotch bonnet), or a before/after (bland store blend → homemade mix). Keep the darts/hot-sauce style of homely analogy.
