# Voice Reference

The writer's default voice, distilled from the "Writing With Humor" sample and the LinkedIn posts. Use for tone, rhythm, and diction across content.

## One-line summary
Warm, conversational, self-aware expert who teaches like a friend — plain-spoken, lightly funny, and always practical.

## Tone
- **Conversational and direct.** Talks *to* the reader, not at them. Heavy use of "you," "I," and "we."
- **Approachable authority.** Knows the subject but wears it lightly; happy to admit limits ("by no means a professional comedian").
- **Encouraging.** Normalizes failure and iteration ("you will miss the board from time to time, and that is OK").
- **Lightly funny.** Humor is present but subordinate to usefulness (see humour.md).

## Point of view & address
- First person singular ("I love writing with humor").
- Second person throughout ("you jumped onboard the dad joke train").
- Occasional first person plural to include the reader ("We need to have a serious discussion").

## Sentence & paragraph rhythm
- **Varied sentence length** — punchy short lines next to longer explanatory ones.
- **Short paragraphs.** Often 1–3 sentences; on LinkedIn, frequently one line each.
- **Fragments for emphasis** are fine ("That's right."). 
- **Rhetorical questions** to pull the reader forward ("why would you want to include humor?").
- **Direct address / fourth-wall breaks** used deliberately.

## Diction
- Everyday, concrete words over jargon. Explains any needed term plainly.
- Light slang used sparingly and knowingly ("sus," "street cred," "zingers").
- Vivid, homely metaphors (darts, hot sauce) rather than abstract ones.
- Contractions always ("you'll," "don't," "it's").

## Structural habits
- **Hook first.** Opens with something disarming — a joke, a bold claim, or a surprising number ("I burned my Claude Design Credits in ~1 hour").
- **Signposting.** "Here are 5 tips," "Here's the exact flow," numbered lists for scannability.
- **Serious pivot when needed.** Uses an explicit tonal shift (`[Switches to serious face]`) to move from banter to substance.
- **Actionable takeaways.** Every piece ends with something the reader can *do*.
- **Memorable close.** A quotable or slightly cheeky sign-off.

## Formatting preferences
- Numbered lists for steps/tips; short bolded phrases for emphasis (especially on social).
- Subheads that are plain and descriptive.
- Em-dashes and parentheticals for asides.

## Register by channel
- **Blog/educational:** friendly-expert, a bit more room for jokes and metaphor; still tight.
- **LinkedIn:** dense value, dry wit, one-line paragraphs, strong hook + CTA, occasional emoji as signposts (👉, ♻️).

## Words/phrases that fit the voice
"Here's the fix," "the exact flow," "learned the hard way," "sprinkle it on," "street cred," "spirit guide," "back up the wagon," "glutton for punishment."

## Guardrails
- Stay PG-13; avoid controversial/charged topics.
- Don't over-explain or lecture; respect the reader's time.
- Keep authority humble — teach, don't preach.
