# Beef Jerky Time & Temperature Guide

Making safe, properly dried beef jerky requires hitting the right internal temperature -- 160°F (71°C) -- for long enough to kill harmful bacteria. Drying time ranges from 3-6 hours depending on method, slice thickness, and temperature. This guide gives you a complete time and temperature reference for every common jerky-making method.

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Key numbers:</strong> Dehydrate at 160°F for 4-6 hours. Oven at 175°F for 3-5 hours. Smoker at 160-180°F for 4-6 hours. Always test with the bend test before pulling jerky from the heat.</p>

## Beef Jerky Time & Temperature Chart

| Method | Temperature | Time (1/4-inch slices) | Time (3/8-inch slices) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food dehydrator | 160°F (71°C) | 4-6 hours | 6-8 hours |
| Oven (with door propped) | 175°F (80°C) | 3-5 hours | 5-7 hours |
| Pellet/electric smoker | 165-180°F | 4-6 hours | 6-8 hours |
| Charcoal smoker (indirect) | 160-175°F | 4-8 hours | Varies |
| Air fryer (dehydrator mode) | 160°F | 2-4 hours | 3-5 hours |

## USDA Safety Requirements

The USDA's recommendation for jerky safety is to reach an **internal temperature of 160°F** throughout the meat to destroy *Salmonella* and *E. coli*. There are two ways to achieve this:

1. **Marinate and dry normally**, then finish in a 275°F oven for 10 minutes to ensure the internal temp is reached
2. **Pre-heat the meat to 160°F** before dehydrating: simmer marinated strips in marinade for 5 minutes before placing in dehydrator

Most experienced home jerky makers rely on proper drying at 160°F+ to handle food safety naturally, but the USDA step-heating method is recommended for anyone serving high-risk individuals (elderly, pregnant, immunocompromised).

## How Temperature Affects Jerky Texture

- **Too low (below 145°F):** Safety risk, slow drying, bacteria can survive
- **145-155°F:** Slow, gentle drying; more tender result but longer time
- **160°F (target):** Standard dehydrating temperature; best balance of safety and texture
- **170-180°F:** Faster drying; can case-harden exterior (dry outside, moist inside)
- **Above 200°F:** Cooks the meat rather than dehydrates it; jerky becomes tough/brittle

## Drying Times by Slice Thickness

Slice thickness is the single biggest factor in drying time. Always slice consistently:

| Slice Thickness | Texture Result | Drying Time at 160°F |
|---|---|---|
| 1/8 inch | Very thin, crispy | 2-3 hours |
| 1/4 inch | Standard chewy | 4-6 hours |
| 3/8 inch | Thick, meaty | 6-8 hours |
| 1/2 inch | Very thick (not recommended) | 8-10+ hours |

## Marinating Time

Marinating time affects flavor depth, not food safety:

| Marinating Time | Result |
|---|---|
| 4-6 hours | Light flavor, not fully penetrated |
| 8-12 hours | Standard -- good flavor throughout |
| 18-24 hours | Maximum flavor, very tender |
| 48 hours | Overly soft texture; not recommended |

## The Bend Test for Doneness

Temperature probes are difficult to use on thin jerky slices. Use the **bend test** instead:

1. Remove a strip from the dehydrator
2. Let it cool 2-3 minutes
3. Bend it at a 90° angle
4. **Done:** Bends and cracks, possibly tears slightly. No moist areas visible at the bend
5. **Underdone:** Feels soft, rubbery, or bends completely without cracking
6. **Overdone:** Snaps completely in half without any flex; very dry and hard
