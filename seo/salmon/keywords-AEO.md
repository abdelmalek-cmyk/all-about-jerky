## CRITICAL — FISH SAFETY (completely different from meat; this is the E-E-A-T differentiator)

Salmon is NOT beef/poultry. Get this genuinely right and cite **FDA/USDA**:

1. **PARASITE RISK / FREEZE STEP.** Raw salmon can carry parasites. The FDA "sushi-grade"/parasite-destruction guidance: freeze at **-4°F (-20°C) for 7 days**, OR **-31°F (-35°C) until solid then stored 15 hours**. For any jerky that isn't cooked to a killing temperature (especially COLD-smoked or low-temp dried), the freeze step is the safety control. This is the single most important safety point and most competitors omit it.
2. **COOKING TEMP.** USDA safe finished temp for fish is **145°F** (lower than beef 160/poultry 165). Hot-dried/hot-smoked salmon jerky brought to 145°F is cooked-safe.
3. **HOT vs COLD SMOKED.** Hot-smoked = cooked (safe). Cold-smoked = not cooked (needs the freeze step). Make the distinction explicit — the `cold smoked salmon jerky` (260) and `smoked salmon strips` (590) searchers need this.
4. **FATTY FISH = SHORTER SHELF LIFE + REFRIGERATION.** Salmon's oils go rancid faster than lean meat. `how long does salmon jerky last` (30), `does salmon jerky need to be refrigerated` (20) — answer honestly: refrigerate, shorter shelf life than beef jerky.

Do NOT copy the beef 160°F or poultry 165°F rule. Fish safety is its own thing (145°F + the freeze step for raw applications). Cite sources.
